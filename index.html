Is your business managed by Large Corporates Division?

What is a 'Large Corporate' for eInvoicing purposes?
For Phase One, a business will be considered a large corporate for eInvoicing purposes if they are:
  • a VAT-registered business whose tax affairs are managed by Large Corporates Division in Revenue on 1 January 2027; and
  • established, or have a fixed establishment, in Ireland.

All businesses receiving eInvoices

Based on the answers you have provided, your business will be required to receive eInvoices from 1 November 2028. This requirement applies to your business regardless of its VAT registration status. The definition of a business is available on the All businesses page of the Revenue website.

If your only requirement during these phases is to receive eInvoices, you do not need to take any action at this time. More guidance will be provided in due course.

How to prepare

As your business is required to receive eInvoices from 1 November 2028, you should:

  • stay informed. Join our mailing list to receive the latest updates and insights on eInvoicing and VAT Modernisation.
  • ensure you have an active digital certificate for Revenue Online Services (ROS). Revenue will use this channel for direct business communications.
  • attend our communication events, e.g. webinars, which will be announced on www.revenue.ie.
  • review your current invoicing or accounting systems.
  • plan for any necessary updates or upgrades.

VAT registered large corporates

Based on the answers you have provided, your business is a VAT registered large corporate.

If your tax affairs continue to be managed by Large Corporates Division in Revenue on 1 January 2027, from 1 November 2028 you will be required to:

  • issue structured eInvoices for taxable domestic business to business (B2B) transactions.
  • report a subset of the relevant eInvoice data to Revenue in real time for those domestic transactions.
  • ensure your business can receive eInvoices from your suppliers.

How to prepare

To prepare your business for Phase One, you should:

  • stay informed. Join our mailing list to receive the latest updates and insights on eInvoicing and VAT Modernisation.
  • ensure you have an active digital certificate for Revenue Online Services. Revenue will use this channel for direct business communications.
  • attend our communication events, e.g. webinars, which will be announced on this hub.
  • review your current invoicing or accounting systems.
  • ensure that you have all relevant data required for the businesses in your supply chain.
  • engage with your software provider to discuss technical readiness.
  • ensure that your internal teams and departments understand the new requirements.
  • plan for any necessary updates or upgrades.

If your business is also engaged in cross-border EU business to business (B2B) trade, you will also be impacted by the introduction of the European Union’s VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative. Ensure you stay informed on the ViDA requirements as they are announced.

VAT registered business with EU business to business (B2B) trade

Based on the answers you have provided, your business is a VAT registered business engaged in cross-border EU B2B trade (zero-rated).

The domestic obligation will extend to all VAT-registered businesses engaged in taxable cross-border EU B2B trade, who benefit from the zero-rate goods or reverse charge services VAT arrangements.

From 1 November 2028 you are required to:

  • ensure your business can receive eInvoices from other Irish businesses.
